The Circulatory system

The circulatory system is the body’s transport network. Its main job is to continuously pump blood around the body so that cells receive the materials they need to survive. Blood carries oxygen, glucose, nutrients, hormones, and other important substances to body cells. At the same time, it removes waste products such as carbon dioxide (CO₂).
Think of the circulatory system like a giant highway system. The heart acts as the central pump station, while the blood vessels are the roads that allow blood to travel everywhere in the body.
Without the circulatory system, body cells would not receive enough oxygen or nutrients to produce energy, and harmful wastes would build up.

Functions:
1. Pumps blood throughout the body.
2. Delivers oxygen to cells.
3. Transports glucose and nutrients
4. Removes CO2 and wastes.
5. Connects the body system together

The heart

A strong muscular organ that constantly pumps blood across the body and oxygenates deoxygenated blood.

The heart is divided into four chambers. The two top ones are called atriums and the two bottom chambers are called ventricles.

Keep in mind!

Heart diagrams are shown as if you're looking at a person from the front. So, the left part of the heart is on your right, and the right is on your left.

Blood vessels

Blood travels through mainly three types of blood vessels: arteries, veins and capillaries.

Arteries

Arteries carry blood away from the heart to the body. The blood they carry is oxygenated and is delivered to the various parts of the body.

Key features:
1. Thick muscular walls
2. High blood pressure
3. Usually carries oxygenated blood (pulmonary arteries)

Veins

Veins carry blood to the heart. The blood here is deoxygenated.

Key features:
1. Thinner walls than arteries
2. Contains valves to prevent backward flow because of low pressure.
3. Usually carries deoxygenated blood (except pulmonary veins)

Interesting

Deoxygenated blood being blue is a common misconception. It is actually dark red. Veins can look blue through the skin because of how light passes through them.

Capillaries

Capillaries are tiny blood vessels that connect arteries and veins. This is where exchange happens between the blood and body cells.

Key features:
1. Moving oxygen into cells
2. CO2 into blood
3. Delivers nutrients
4. Collects blood waste

Blood flow pathway

Blood does not travel around the body in one big loop. It goes through the heart twice on every full circuit, which is why we call it a double circulatory system. One loop is short and goes to the lungs to pick up oxygen. The other is long and delivers that oxygen to the rest of the body. The two circuits are the pulmonary circuit and the systemic circuit.

The pulmonary circuit

This is the short loop. Its job is to take deoxygenated blood to the lungs, where where the blood is oxygenated.

1. Deoxygenated blood returns from the body through the vena cava.
2. It enters the right atrium.
3. It passes down through a valve into the right ventricle.
4. The right ventricle pumps it out through the pulmonary artery to the lungs.
5. In the lungs it drops off CO2 and picks up oxygen.
6. Oxygenated blood returns to the heart through the pulmonary veins.

The systemic circuit

This is the long loop, and its job is to deliver oxygen and nutrients to every cell in the body.

1. Oxygenated blood from the lungs enters the left atrium.
2. It passes down through a valve into the left ventricle.
3. The left ventricle pumps it out through the aorta at high pressure.
4. Arteries carry it to the organs, then split into capillaries.
5. Cells take the oxygen and nutrients, and pass back CO2 and waste.
6. Veins carry the deoxygenated blood back to the vena cava, and the whole thing starts again.

Things worth remembering

• The right side of the heart handles deoxygenated blood; the left side handles oxygenated blood. The septum keeps them apart.
• Blood always enters the heart at an atrium and leaves from a ventricle.
• The left ventricle has the thickest wall, because it has to push blood all the way around the body — the right ventricle only has to reach the lungs.
• The pulmonary artery is the one artery carrying deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary vein is the one vein carrying oxygenated blood. They are the exception to the usual rule.
• Valves make sure blood only ever moves one way.

Gas Exchange

Gas exchange is the process through which oxygen moves from the air in the lungs into the blood, while carbon dioxide (CO₂) moves from the blood into the lungs to be breathed out.
Gas exchange occurs in the alveoli, which are tiny air sacs inside the lungs. The alveoli are surrounded by tiny blood vessels called capillaries.

How does it happen?

1. Deoxygenated blood travels from the right ventricle to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.
2. In the lungs, the blood reaches the capillaries surrounding the alveoli.
3. Oxygen moves from the alveoli into the blood.
4. CO₂ moves from the blood into the alveoli.
5. The CO₂ is breathed out when you exhale.
6. The newly oxygenated blood travels back to the heart through the pulmonary veins and enters the left atrium.
